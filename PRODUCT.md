# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, engineering leads, collaborators, and technical peers visiting a personal portfolio to understand whether Sanskar can build reliable backend, AI, automation, and infrastructure systems. Most arrive with limited time, skim for proof, and need confidence quickly: what he builds, what scale it survived, and how to contact him.

## Product Purpose

The site is a personal brand surface for Sanskar Jaiswal. It should convert technical credibility into trust: show real production impact, convey a distinct operator personality, and make the path to projects, writing, and contact obvious. Success means a visitor leaves remembering Sanskar as the Python/backend engineer who builds weird reliable systems and can prove it.

## Brand Personality

Mechanical, candid, reliable. The voice should be sharp and specific without startup cliché: runbooks, production scars, homelab-as-staging, measurable savings, and sober confidence. Humor is allowed when it proves taste; cringe, corporate polish, and generic portfolio enthusiasm are not.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolio templates with numbered section scaffolds, repeated uppercase eyebrows, identical cards, and vague “passionate full-stack” copy.
- Terminal cosplay where monospace and ASCII overwhelm readability or make the site feel like a theme instead of a portfolio.
- Emoji dashboards and Notion-style homelab grids that make infrastructure feel lightweight.
- Over-designed agency/luxury editorial aesthetics that hide the engineering proof.
- Broken production telemetry, fake links, placeholder data, or console noise that contradicts the reliability story.

## Design Principles

1. Proof before decoration: every visual flourish should support evidence of reliability, scale, or taste.
2. Practice what the copy promises: production errors, broken links, poor fallbacks, and accessibility misses are brand damage.
3. Make the homelab relevant: show it as a live systems-thinking project, not a separate hobby section.
4. Distinct without costume: use technical motifs sparingly and precisely; avoid both generic minimalism and terminal cosplay.
5. Mobile visitors need a direct path: contact and core sections must remain reachable without a long-scroll hunt.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast, semantic links/buttons, visible focus states, keyboard-accessible navigation, reduced-motion fallbacks for reveal/marquee effects, and clean behavior when third-party APIs fail. Text must stay readable at mobile widths and browser zoom.
